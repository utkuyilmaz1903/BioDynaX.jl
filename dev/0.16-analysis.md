# 0.16 analysis: what should and should not be separable

Written before any code, to set the expectations that the study of Phase 4
tests. The model is `du_i/dt = P_i(u) − D_i(u)·u_i`; an unknown term is one
`D_i`, learned by a network that sees only that term's regulators. With
several unknown terms each network sees its own regulators, and the only
thing that ties the terms together is the trajectory the whole system
produces. So the question is always the same: does the trajectory constrain
each `D_i` separately, or can a change in one be paid for by a change in
another?

Four cases, from the easiest to the hardest.

**Non-adjacent nodes, both observed.** `D_A` acts on node A, `D_B` on node
B, and neither node regulates the other's term. The observed trajectory of
A pins `D_A·A` through `dA/dt − P_A`, and likewise for B. The two terms are
coupled only through the known kinetics that connect the nodes, and a
compensation would have to travel along that path: raising `D_A` lowers A,
which changes whatever A feeds, which would then have to be undone by
`D_B`. Because A itself is observed, that route is closed: the fit pays for
the wrong A directly. Expectation: separable; per-term support recovery at
the single-term level, and the cross-term sensitivity cosine well below one.
The cost of the second unknown term should be the wider parameter search,
not a loss of identifiability.

**Adjacent nodes, A regulates B.** `D_B` takes A as input, and A's trajectory
depends on `D_A`. A shift in `D_A` changes A, which changes the input of
`D_B`; `D_B` can absorb part of that by reshaping its response to A. The
trajectory of A is still observed, so `D_A` itself is still pinned by
`dA/dt`; what becomes soft is the *functional form* of `D_B`, because the
network for B sees a moved input and adjusts. The failure mode in the data
is not a divergent fit but a good fit with the wrong `D_B` support: extra
terms, a lower Hill exponent, or a scale that drifts against the neighbour's.
Expectation: the fit residual stays low, `D_A` recovers as in the single-term
case, and `D_B` degrades first. The cross-term cosine is the diagnostic that
should rise here, because the sensitivity of the trajectory to a scale change
of `D_A` and to a scale change of `D_B` overlap along A's trajectory.

**One node unobserved.** If B is not observed, `D_B·B` is only constrained
through B's effect on A. Then `D_B` is only as identifiable as B's production
and destruction together, and the same production/destruction scale problem
the single-term diagnostic already flags for one unknown now sits inside the
pair: `D_B` can be scaled up while B's production is scaled up, leaving A's
trajectory unchanged. Expectation: not separable in general; the per-term
Fisher diagnostic for B should flag it and the cross-term cosine between
`D_A` and `D_B` should be high. This is the p53 situation of 0.13 with a
second unknown term added, and the analysis predicts it will not improve.

**Shared regulators.** Two terms whose graph-local libraries contain the
same regulator (A and C both regulated by B) are separable as long as the
targets A and C are both observed: each `D` multiplies its own observed
target, so the data separate them even though their inputs coincide. What
they share is the sampling grid, so their discovered supports will show the
same extra terms when B's observed range is narrow. Expectation: separable,
with correlated extras rather than compensation.

**What compensation looks like, operationally.** Two terms compensate when
one learned rate is biased high and the other biased low over the observed
regulator range while the total loss is unchanged: the signed relative bias
of each term against its true rate has opposite signs, and the fit residual
does not tell them apart. Phase 4 measures exactly this: per term, the signed
mean relative error of the learned rate on the sampling grid, and per run,
whether the two signs are opposite with magnitudes above the single-term
control's error.

**The diagnostic.** For each pair, the same construction as the existing
scale check: perturb the output of one term by a multiplicative factor
`1 ± δ`, simulate, take the finite-difference sensitivity of the observed
trajectory, and compute the cosine between the two terms' sensitivity vectors
over the observed entries. A cosine near one says a scale change of one term
looks, in the data, like a scale change of the other; then the pair's scales
are not separately determined and the discovered coefficients inherit that
uncertainty. It says nothing about the functional form beyond the scale, and
it is a local statement at the fitted parameters, as the existing warning is.
